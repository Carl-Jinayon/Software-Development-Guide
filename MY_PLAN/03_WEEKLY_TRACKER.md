## The five non-negotiable rules

1. **Never start a day by reading.** Start by writing something.
2. **Every day ends with a commit.** Even five lines.
3. **One day a week is a "no new topics" day** — Sunday.
4. **Never skip two days in a row.** Momentum beats intensity.
5. **The maintenance slot never exceeds 30 minutes.** If it grows, I have started a second spine.

## Month checkpoints

**At the end of each month, honestly answer:**

1. Can I do everything on this month's topic **without looking anything up**?
2. Have I **shipped** something that runs?
3. Is there **GitHub evidence** of it?
4. Could I explain what I built to a non-technical person in **2 minutes**?

**If #1 or #2 is no → delay the next month and fix it.**

Rushing forward accumulates a hollow foundation, and hollow foundations collapse at exactly the moment I need them: during an interview.

## Exams, holidays, and life happening

**The plan must survive bad weeks, or it is not a plan.**

| Situation | Response |
|---|---|
| **Finals week** | Drop to 30 min/day. Just commit. Do not break the streak. |
| **A bad day** | Do 15 minutes. Commit. The streak matters more than the hours. |
| **A bad week** | Monday: restart. Do not try to "make up" lost time. |
| **Two bad weeks** | Reduce scope. Ship a smaller thing. Momentum is the whole game. |
| **Illness** | Skip. Do not guilt yourself. Resume the next day. |
| **Travel** | 30 minutes on a laptop, or nothing. Do not abandon. |

**The recovery rule:** after any gap, the next day's only requirement is *one commit*. Not a full session. One commit. That is enough to restart momentum.

## The review question

**Every Sunday, ask yourself one question:**

> *"If a recruiter looked at my GitHub right now, what would they conclude I can do?"*

If the answer is vague, the next week's deliverable should make it specific.
